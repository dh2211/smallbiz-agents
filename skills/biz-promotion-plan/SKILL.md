# Promotion plan

Plan a promotion the owner can run with the time and money they actually have.

## Ask first

1. The goal in the owner's words: clear old stock, launch a product, bring back regulars, fill quiet days.
2. The offer the owner is willing to honor: discount, bundle, freebie, event. Ask for exact amounts and end dates.
3. Budget and time: money for ads or printing, and hours per week the owner can spend.
4. Channels they already use: shop window, email list, social accounts, marketplace, local groups.
5. Dates: start, end, and any holidays or local events nearby.

## Steps

1. Restate the goal as one number the owner can count: "sell 40 of the summer hats", "15 returning customers on Tuesdays". Use the owner's own figures; don't predict results.
2. Write the offer precisely: what, how much off, who qualifies, start and end date, limits. Check the arithmetic on any discount.
3. Pick at most three channels from the ones the owner already uses, and say why each fits.
4. Build a dated calendar: what goes out, where, and who does it. Include prep (signs, photos, stock check) before launch.
5. Draft the key messages, one per channel. Use the `biz-social-post` skill for posts.
6. Add how to measure: what to count, where, and when to check.
7. Add an honesty check: the offer is real, the end date is real, any "limited" claim matches real stock, and the promotion follows the platform's and local advertising rules `[owner to confirm]`.

## Output

Goal, offer, budget, channels, calendar table (date, task, channel, who), key messages, how to measure, honesty check. Keep it to one or two pages.

## Guardrails

- Facts from the owner: use only the products, specs, prices, and terms the owner gives you. Write `[owner to confirm]` where something is missing. Never invent features, materials, certifications, origins, health or environmental claims, stock levels, awards, or customer quotes.
- Money: the owner checks every price, quantity, discount, tax rate, total, and payment term. Show the arithmetic. Mark tax rates and legally required invoice details `[owner to confirm]` unless the owner gave them. This is not accounting, tax, or legal advice.
- Honest marketing: no fake reviews, no posing as a customer, no reviews of competitors, and no fake urgency or scarcity ("only 2 left", countdowns) unless the owner confirms it is true. Reply only to real reviews, as the business.
- Customer privacy: work without customer names, addresses, contact details, order numbers, or payment details. If the owner pastes them, replace them with placeholders such as `[customer]` and say that you did. Never put customer details in a public reply.
- The owner decides: you draft. Nothing is sent, posted, or published automatically. Safety, hygiene, and legal steps in procedures must be checked by the owner against the rules that apply to the business.
